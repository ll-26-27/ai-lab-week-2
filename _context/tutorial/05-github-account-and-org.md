# 05. GitHub account and the course organization

**Why now:** this repository, `tdm155ai/tdm155ai-week-2`, is private. It is visible only to members of the `tdm155ai` organization on GitHub. Until your invitation is accepted, cloning it on the next page fails with "repository not found," and the invitation needs one of us to send it. So post your username (5.3) as early in this step as you can, and let the invitation land while you finish the rest.

## 5.1 Create an account, or sign in

Go to https://github.com and sign in, or create an account.

- **Username.** It will be public and it will be on everything you push. Pick one you'd put on a résumé. You can't easily change it later.
- **Email.** Use your Harvard College email, the same one as everything else today, so the invitation reaches you. You can add a personal address later under Settings → Emails so the account outlives your Harvard one.
- **Plan.** Free. Don't buy anything.

If you made an account in week 1, just sign in and check you remember the password.

## 5.2 Two-factor authentication

GitHub requires two-factor authentication (2FA) for most accounts and will prompt you to set it up, sometimes right away, sometimes after a few days. When it does: choose an authenticator app on your phone (Google Authenticator, 1Password, Duo, or the built-in one in iOS Passwords) or a passkey. Save the recovery codes it shows you somewhere you'll find them again. If you skip this now, expect the prompt to come back.

## 5.3 Tell us your username

Post your GitHub username in the course Slack channel, on its own line, in this form:

```
github: your-username
```

One of us adds you to the organization. The invitation goes to the email on your GitHub account and also appears on GitHub itself.

## 5.4 Accept the invitation

Either:

- open the email from GitHub and click **View invitation**, or
- go to https://github.com/tdm155ai and look for the yellow **You've been invited** banner at the top.

Click **Accept**. If GitHub asks you to confirm your password or 2FA, do that.

## 5.5 Confirm it worked

Open https://github.com/tdm155ai/tdm155ai-week-2 in the browser.

- If you see the repository (a README, a `_context` folder with the tutorial inside, a `utils` folder), you're in. Carry on to the [next page](06-github-sign-in-and-clone.md) to clone it.
- If you see a **404** page, you aren't in the organization yet. Check the email and the banner again, or tell us.

## What an organization is, and public versus private

Your personal GitHub account owns your repositories. An organization is a second kind of owner, shared by a group, with its own repositories and a membership list. `tdm155ai` is the course's organization. Some of its repos are public, which means anyone on the internet can read them: the week 1 image experiment at https://github.com/tdm155ai/week-1-image-experiment is one, and its GitHub Pages site is how we published that work. Others, like this one, are private to the members. Over the term you'll decide, piece by piece, what of your own work is public, private, or shared only with the course; this is the first place that distinction becomes concrete.
