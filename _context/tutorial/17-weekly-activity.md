# 17. This week's activity

**Assigned Wednesday, September 16 · Due Monday, September 21, by noon · Submit in Slack**

Same pattern as last week, and every week: at least thirty uninterrupted minutes continuing one or more of today's activities on your own machine, then at least five minutes of unprepared talking on your phone. Practices, not products. Unfinished is fine; strange is good.

## 1. At least thirty uninterrupted minutes

Pick **one or more** of these and keep going, on your own machine, for at least thirty minutes without interruption. Phone face down, other tabs closed, timer set. Everything here uses only what you installed today.

1. **Your own three words.** Run `batch-images.mjs` with a prompt of your own choosing, three words or fewer, across three models, four runs each. Then change one word and run it again. Post both grids and three sentences on what moved and what didn't. (Page 13 has the command; `node utils/list-image-models.mjs` and `node utils/list-fal-models.mjs` have the models.)
2. **A different fact.** Use `batch-text.mjs` with `--expect` to ask five models something with one checkable answer that isn't multiplication: a date, a count, a conversion, a line of a poem. Post the results table and say which models were wrong and how.
3. **A folder of your own.** Make a new folder with some material of yours (notes, a draft, a transcript, photos). Write a ten-line `AGENTS.md` and the one-line `CLAUDE.md` that imports it (the full version at the end of tutorial page 09 is a model; yours will be shorter). Open the folder in Claude Code and in Codex and ask each what it knows about the project. Post the `AGENTS.md` and what each said.
4. **A skill.** Take something you did twice today and write it as a skill: a folder, a `SKILL.md`, a description that says when to use it. `family-batch` in the repo is the model. Invoke it. Post the `SKILL.md` and one result.
5. **Stills from footage.** Point `stills.mjs` at any video you have, last week's studio footage or something off your phone, and make a contact sheet. Then pull one still at a timestamp you chose on purpose. Post the sheet and the still and say why that frame.
6. **One real task in the browser or on the screen.** Give Claude in Chrome, or computer use where your account has it, one task you'd otherwise do by hand, and watch every step. Post what you asked, what it asked you, and where it needed help.
7. **Read with AI.** Give a model a whole document, not a summary of one: the Claude Code docs, a paper behind a news story you saw this week, this repo's whole `_context/glossary/` folder. Ask it a question the introduction can't answer. Post the question, the answer, and whether the answer held up when you checked the source.

When the timer goes off, **post whatever you have to the course Slack channel**, in whatever state it's in. Say in a line which option you chose and roughly how long you spent. If you used API calls, say roughly how many.

## 2. A five-minute reflection, on your phone

Record **at least five minutes** of yourself talking, on your phone, wherever you are. No script, no retakes, no editing. Three things:

- **what you did in class this week**,
- **something that stuck with you**: a wrong number, an image, a command, a line someone said, and
- **your piece**: the interesting choices you made while working on it, and what you learned, including what broke.

Post the video to Slack alongside the piece.

## How this is evaluated

The arc of the week: class, the Slack discussion, the piece, the reflection. Not the polish of the piece. A rough result with a clear account of what went wrong and what you'd try next is a strong week.

## A note on keys

The class key is for this week's work. Keep the batches small (three models, four runs is plenty), never paste the key anywhere, and if a run fails with a message about credit or limits, stop and post the message rather than retrying.
